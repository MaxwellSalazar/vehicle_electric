\abstract{Light electric vehicle prototypes with vehicle-integrated photovoltaics are often evaluated using low-cost telemetry whose measurement chain is rarely documented. This study characterizes field use and audits the telemetry of a single-seat prototype equipped with a 200 Wp photovoltaic array, a 2 kW brushless DC (BLDC) motor, and a 48 V gel-battery bank in Sangolquí, Ecuador (approximately 2500 m above sea level). Of 4277 GPS and electrical records collected in July and August 2026, 3052 records from 11 dates were retained. Eight sessions with at least 100 observations (3029 records) were summarized by session using a block bootstrap. The data revealed a repeatedly traveled route with an extent of approximately 1.4 km (mean speed, 14.7--16.0 km/h) and confined circuits with displacements below 0.15 km (7.7--8.7 km/h). The estimated speed matched the value derived from GPS coordinates (RMSE, 0.003 km/h); therefore, this comparison is not an independent validation. Motor current was bounded at $\pm 6.55$ A (6.9\% of observations), had a session-dependent baseline (mean values ranging from $+0.9$ to $-2.2$ A), and did not respond to speed ($R^2=0.003$) or acceleration ($\rho=-0.01$). The channel labeled as the panel current was bounded at $\pm 3.28$ A, with 34\% zero readings. The current measurement chain does not support estimates of energy consumption or photovoltaic generation; minimum instrumentation requirements are proposed.}

\section{Introduction}

Vehicle-integrated photovoltaics (VIPV) can turn vehicle surfaces into electricity generators and, in some configurations, supplement the energy stored in the battery. Reviews and modeling studies indicate that VIPV potential depends on active surface area, orientation, shading, temperature, irradiance, and usage patterns; therefore, design estimates cannot replace measurements made on the vehicle \citep{ref-commault,ref-sierra,ref-hoth,ref-kouzelis}. Field studies that quantify the output of vehicle-mounted modules require measurements of irradiance on the vehicle body and module temperature, together with a description of the driving profile \citep{ref-wetzel,ref-ng,ref-ota-irradiance,ref-ota-driving,ref-ota-curve}.

A growing body of research on light electric vehicles uses low-cost data loggers (GPS and voltage and current sensors) to describe real-world use and estimate energy consumption. These studies assume that each channel measures the quantity indicated by its label, with an adequate range and zero offset. However, the measurement chain is rarely documented, and common problems in field records (zero readings, spurious peaks, saturation, and zero drift) are addressed using filters that are seldom described in detail \citep{ref-bhavsar,ref-alwreikat}. Voltage- and current-sensor errors directly affect derived quantities \citep{ref-hu,ref-zheng}. Real-world driving cycles and GPS data analysis have been used to characterize the use of electric and light vehicles \citep{ref-pfriem,ref-brady,ref-uberti,ref-gurusamy}, but less attention has been paid to the validity of the accompanying electrical signals. Field data for solar-assisted light vehicles at equatorial latitudes and high elevations are also scarce, as is documentation of how telemetry signals from low-cost prototypes are assessed; both are relevant to vehicle development in the Andean region.

The analysis uses field records from a light electric vehicle prototype developed at the Instituto Tecnológico Superior Rumiñahui (ISTER) in Sangolquí, Ecuador, together with the vehicle's technical specifications. Specifically, it (i) characterizes GPS-tracked routes and speeds in an Andean setting, (ii) assesses whether the available electrical telemetry supports estimates of energy consumption or photovoltaic generation, and (iii) derives minimum measurement-chain requirements from the observed data-quality problems.
The vehicle's solar performance is not evaluated because the available data do not support such an assessment.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Vehicle and Documented Specifications}

The prototype is a single-seat vehicle with a steel chassis, chain drive to the rear axle, and a mid-drive 48 V, 2 kW nominal BLDC motor. The motor's maximum design current is 46.3 A (57.9 A including the protection margin stated in the specifications). Two flexible BougeRV Arch100 modules (100 Wp, 100\,$\times$\,60 cm) connected in series form an array with $V_{\mathrm{mp}}=34.2$ V, $V_{\mathrm{oc}}=39.6$ V, and $I_{\mathrm{sc}}=6.16$ A. The array is connected to a 300 W DC HOUSE MPPT boost converter with an input range of 13--65 V. The energy-storage bank comprises four 12 V, 60 Ah gel batteries connected in series (valve-regulated lead-acid batteries with gelled electrolyte). The vehicle mass is not reported in the specifications. Figure~\ref{fig:prototipo} presents the vehicle layout, while Figure~\ref{fig:unifilar} shows the electrical system and the channels recorded by the telemetry.

The specifications also include calculated values for available power, efficiency, range, and design solar conditions (latitude $-0.31^\circ$; 5.08 peak-sun hours). These are design values, not experimental results. The experiment did not measure irradiance, module temperature, battery state of charge, or energy at the battery terminals.

\subsection{Data}

Two workbooks containing movement records (July and August 2026) and one consolidated workbook containing the retained data and the exclusion reason for each record were used. The movement workbooks contain 4277 records (3714 from July and 563 from August), previously filtered to estimated speeds between 1 and 120 km/h. The variables are timestamp (typically sampled every 5 s), battery voltage, voltage and current in channels labeled as panel and motor, GPS coordinates, distance increment, and estimated speed. The coordinates are not reproduced as georeferenced tracks; routes are shown in relative local coordinates.

The consolidated workbook contains 3052 retained records (71.4\%) from 11 dates; 1225 records (28.6\%) were excluded. A record was excluded if its battery voltage was zero or outside 29.91--64.95 V, its panel voltage was zero or outside 4.46--54.73 V, or its panel current exceeded 100 A in absolute value. Current signs were kept. A date was considered to have adequate coverage if it contained at least 100 observations. Eight dates met this criterion (3029 records). The other three dates, each with 1--18 records, are listed in Table~\ref{tab:sesiones} but were not included in comparisons between sessions. This threshold was used only to organize the analysis; it does not measure data quality or representativeness. Its effect is examined in the sensitivity analysis.

\subsection{Analysis}

\textbf{Movement summary.} For each session, the summaries included the number of records, the sum of distance increments, mean and maximum speed, median speed, and interquartile range (IQR). Because movement-based filtering had already been applied and excluded records leave gaps, the sum of the increments may not equal the total distance traveled. Route geometry was described by the maximum displacement from the first point and by the north--south and east--west spans in local coordinates.

\textbf{Speed consistency and driving profile.} The reported speed was compared with the speed derived from coordinates (Haversine distance between consecutive records divided by the time interval, for intervals of 4--6 s) using bias, Bland--Altman limits of agreement, and RMSE. Acceleration was calculated as the difference in reported speed between consecutive records (for intervals of 4--6 s). The speed--acceleration distribution and relative positive acceleration (RPA, $\sum v\,a^{+}\Delta t/\sum v\,\Delta t$) were calculated. Micro-trips were defined as segments of at least six records separated by gaps longer than 30 s. The relationship between acceleration and current was assessed using the Spearman coefficient for each session.

\textbf{Electrical signals.} Recorded electrical power was calculated as $P=V_{\mathrm{bat}}I_{\mathrm{motor}}$, preserving the channel sign, and integrated by session as $E=\sum P_i\Delta t_i$ over intervals of up to 30 s; the specific indicator is $E/d$. These quantities are termed signal indicators because sensor polarity, measurement point, and calibration are unknown. Current saturation (observations with $|I|\ge 6.55$ A and the duration of consecutive runs), the current baseline (session mean and mean at low speed), and the panel-channel distribution were quantified. Panel-channel voltage was compared with the array's $V_{\mathrm{mp}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{oc}}$, and its relationship with time of day was examined. A linear regression summarized the relationship between speed and $|P|$. Standard errors were clustered by session (eight clusters), confidence intervals used a $t$ distribution with 7 degrees of freedom, and $R^2$ was reported. The empirical slope $R_{\mathrm{ef}}=-\Delta V_{\mathrm{bat}}/\Delta I_{\mathrm{motor}}$ was estimated using the same correction and session fixed effects. This value describes an observed slope; it is not a measured internal resistance.

\textbf{Uncertainty and sensitivity.} Consecutive observations are autocorrelated, so session-level 95\% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated with a moving-block bootstrap. Each block contained 30 observations, and 4000 resamples were used \citep{ref-kunsch}. Sensitivity checks varied the minimum coverage threshold (50, 100, 200, and 400 records), maximum integration interval (10, 30, 60, and 120 s), minimum speed (1, 3, and 5 km/h), whether saturated observations were excluded, and block length (15, 30, and 60). Between-month comparisons are described using medians and Cliff's $\delta$ \citep{ref-cliff}; no hypothesis tests were applied to autocorrelated observations. Analyses were run in Python with SciPy and statsmodels \citep{ref-scipy,ref-statsmodels}, using a fixed random seed. The code is included in the project materials.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Coverage and Reasons for Exclusion}

Of the 4277 initial records, 3052 were retained (Table~\ref{tab:exclusion}). Zero battery-voltage readings were the most common reason for exclusion (46.7\% of exclusions), followed by zero panel-voltage readings (40.9\%). A further 35.6\% of excluded records failed more than one criterion, which may indicate simultaneous acquisition losses. Four July dates (11, 13, 18, and 20) had no retained records; 247 records from these dates were excluded. The retained proportion for each date ranged from 2\% to 91\% (Supplementary Table S4). Coverage across retained dates was uneven: three dates had fewer than 100 records (21 July: 18; 23 July: 4; 1 August: 1).

\subsection{Routes and Driving Profile}

The eight sessions with adequate coverage comprised two route types (Table~\ref{tab:sesiones}, Figure~\ref{fig:rutas}). From 27 to 31 July, a route with an approximately 1.4 km north--south extent was repeated, with maximum displacements of 1.24--1.25 km and recorded distances of 7.6--13.0 km. The 25 July session and the two August sessions followed confined circuits, with maximum displacements of 0.06--0.13 km. Session mean speed ranged from 14.7 to 16.0 km/h on the repeated route and from 7.7 to 8.7 km/h on the confined circuits (mean across the eight sessions, 12.54 $\pm$ 3.67 km/h; Supplementary Table S1). Median speed was higher in July than in August (13.99 versus 8.80 km/h; Cliff's $\delta=0.375$). However, route type also differed between the months, so the difference cannot be attributed to month alone.

Acceleration above $0.1$ m/s$^2$ occurred in 28.1\% of pairs, while deceleration below $-0.1$ m/s$^2$ occurred in 29.2\%. Speed was nearly constant in the remaining 42.7\%; RPA was 0.10 m/s$^2$. The records therefore describe vehicle movement, not a complete driving cycle that includes stops (Figure~\ref{fig:perfil}). Acceleration levels were modest, as expected for a low-speed light vehicle. Comparisons with reference cycles should therefore be qualitative \citep{ref-andre,ref-tutuianu,ref-brady}.

\subsection{Consistency of Speed with GPS Coordinates}\label{sec:gps}

Reported speeds closely matched speeds calculated from 2414 pairs of GPS coordinates. The bias was $-1.6\times10^{-5}$ km/h, the limits of agreement were $\pm 0.006$ km/h, the RMSE was 0.0029 km/h, and the correlation was 0.99999995. The difference is within the speed's rounding precision of 0.01 km/h; session RMSE values ranged from 0.0028 to 0.0030 km/h. Reported distance increments also matched Haversine distances (RMSE, 0.0029 m). Because both speed values were calculated from the same GPS positions, this comparison checks consistency rather than providing independent validation. It does not rule out GPS positioning or sampling errors \citep{ref-schuessler,ref-zandbergen}. Independent validation would require a wheel odometer or a reference GNSS receiver.

\subsection{Motor Current: Saturation, Baseline, and Response}

Recorded motor current did not exceed $\pm 6.55$ A (Figure~\ref{fig:potencia}). A total of 210 observations (6.9\%) were at this limit. Most were at the negative limit (6.6\% of observations, compared with 0.3\% at the positive limit), and they occurred mainly from 28 to 31 July (8.3--10.9\% per session, compared with 0--0.5\% in the other sessions). Saturation occurred intermittently in 179 episodes. The median episode lasted 5 s (one observation), and the longest lasted 15 s. Only 4.3\% of saturated observations were part of runs of at least three consecutive records. In addition, 9.4\% of observations had exactly zero current, and 18.1\% were within $\pm 0.5$ A. The 6.55 A limit is 14\% of the motor's maximum design current (46.3 A).

Mean current varied by session (Table~\ref{tab:potencia}). It was $+0.38$ A on 25 July and $+0.90$ A on 27 July. From 28 to 31 July, session means ranged from $-1.78$ to $-2.23$ A; in August, they were $-1.11$ and $-1.52$ A. At speeds below 3 km/h ($n=362$), the median was $-1.40$ A (mean, $-2.03$ A). This offset represents a substantial fraction of the recorded range, and the shift between 27 and 28 July is consistent with a change in polarity, zero offset, or configuration between sessions; the data cannot determine which.

Recorded power had an absolute maximum of 357.5 W (17.9\% of the nominal 2 kW), a mean of $-72.8$ W, and 5th and 95th percentiles of $-314$ and $+188$ W. Its relationship with speed was negligible (Figure~\ref{fig:potencia}): $R^2=0.003$ for the regression of $|P|$ on speed (slope, 0.56 W per km/h; 95\% CI with 7 degrees of freedom, 0.08--1.04) and $R^2$ ranged from 0.001 to 0.066 across the eight sessions. Current also did not respond to acceleration: the pooled Spearman coefficient was $-0.01$ ($n=2414$), with session values from $-0.21$ to $+0.09$ (from $-0.06$ to $+0.18$ for $|I|$). Mean current was $-1.7$ A during weak deceleration and acceleration, $-1.1$ A during strong deceleration, and $-1.0$ A during strong acceleration; standard deviations ranged from 3.1 to 3.5 A. A traction-current sensor should show a clear relationship between current and acceleration. Removing saturated observations did not change this result ($R^2=0.0004$).

The energy indicator changed more with the processing settings than with the route. Its mean across sessions was 12.96 Wh/km when the maximum interval was 30 s. With interval limits of 10, 60, and 120 s, the mean was 11.6, 15.2, and 18.8 Wh/km, respectively. With minimum speeds of 1, 3, and 5 km/h, it was 13.0, 11.5, and 10.4 Wh/km, respectively (Supplementary Table S5). Because the indicator also depends on channel polarity and zero offset, it cannot be interpreted as vehicle energy consumption.

\subsection{Panel Channel}

Across the eight analyzed sessions, current in the channel labeled as panel current never exceeded $\pm 3.28$ A. Readings were at this limit in 14.0\% of observations (13.97\% at $+3.28$ A); 34.4\% of readings were zero. The median was 0.54 A, and the 5th and 95th percentiles were $-0.92$ and $+3.28$ A. The observed limit is close to the converter's specified design current injected into the bus (3.51 A). However, these data cannot show whether the limit comes from the converter or the sensor. Panel current was negatively correlated with motor current ($\rho=-0.45$), which suggests that the channels may not be independent or may share a reference.

The most extreme readings came from the 21 July session, which was not one of the eight analyzed sessions. Its 18 retained observations ranged from $-91.9$ to $68.0$ A; 12 exceeded the module's 6.16 A short-circuit current in absolute value. Another 98 records from that date were excluded because they exceeded 100 A, and the largest excluded reading was 3200 A. Such values are implausible in a circuit with a 6.16 A module, and they are outside the analyzed sessions.

Channel voltage ranged from 7.4 to 40.3 V, with a median of 30.0 V (Figure~\ref{fig:panel}a). In 87.3\% of observations, voltage was below the array's $V_{\mathrm{mp}}$ (34.2 V); 12.5\% fell between $V_{\mathrm{mp}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{oc}}$ (39.6 V), and only 0.2\% exceeded $V_{\mathrm{oc}}$. The recorded values fall within the converter's input range (13--65 V), but this alone does not identify where the channel was connected. From 09:00 to 14:00, no clear relationship with time of day was observed. Spearman correlations with time were $-0.05$ for voltage and $-0.02$ for current; median session-level correlations were $-0.08$ and $0.01$, respectively. Hourly median voltages also varied between sessions without a shared trend (Figure~\ref{fig:panel}b). This lack of a pattern could reflect nearly constant irradiance during the measurement period; it does not show that the channel is insensitive to irradiance. Without a pyranometer or reference cell, the readings cannot be linked to incident solar radiation.

\subsection{Empirical Slope of Battery Voltage}

Battery voltage decreased as recorded motor current increased in all eight sessions, with an empirical slope $R_{\mathrm{ef}}$ of 0.15--0.73 $\Omega$ (mean, 0.30 $\Omega$; 95\% $t$-CI, 0.15--0.45; Supplementary Table S2). The pooled estimate with session fixed effects was 0.24 $\Omega$ (95\% CI with clustered errors and 7 degrees of freedom, 0.15--0.33; $R^2=0.15$). This slope is not an internal resistance: it is confounded by state of charge, temperature, and battery relaxation. Because controllers typically draw current depending on the available voltage, causality may operate in either direction. With eight sessions and current not exceeding 6.55 A, the result cannot be extrapolated to traction currents.

\subsection{Sensitivity of Results to Analytical Decisions}

The qualitative results are robust (Supplementary Table S5). The $R^2$ between speed and $|P|$ ranged from 0.0024 to 0.0028 for all minimum-coverage thresholds; saturation ranged from 6.9\% to 9.5\% (and from 7.3\% to 7.6\% with minimum speeds of 3 and 5 km/h); and maximum $|P|$ remained between 355 and 357 W. In contrast, integrated indicators depend on processing: with a minimum coverage of 200 records (five sessions), mean session speed increased from 12.5 to 15.2 km/h and gross energy decreased from 12.96 to 10.13 Wh/km. Doubling the block length from 30 to 60 increased the mean width of the speed CIs from 4.6 to 5.3 km/h.

\subsection{From Findings to Measurement Requirements}

Table~\ref{tab:hallazgos} summarizes the findings, the measurement failure they suggest, and the requirement that would address it. The failures are hypotheses about the measurement chain and should be verified through bench testing and system documentation.

\section{Discussion}

The data characterize prototype use: the repeated route and confined circuits differ in speed and geometry, and the driving profile features low acceleration and speeds predominantly below 20 km/h. Within- and between-session variability supports the view that a standardized cycle alone does not represent these usage patterns and that local cycles should be derived from local measurements \citep{ref-pfriem,ref-brady,ref-uberti}. However, the data cannot establish a complete driving cycle, daily distance, range, or energy consumption. Prior movement-based filtering and gaps longer than 30 s leave too much of the travel record incomplete.

The electrical telemetry cannot support standard performance claims for a vehicle with integrated photovoltaics. Motor-current readings reach a fixed limit, the baseline changes between sessions, and current does not vary with speed or acceleration. The panel-current channel also reaches a fixed limit, and about one-third of its readings are zero. These signals do not show that the photovoltaic system contributed energy. Multiplying voltage by current cannot provide reliable energy estimates unless calibration, synchronization, sign convention, and measurement location are verified \citep{ref-nist,ref-gum}. The energy indicator also changes substantially when the integration interval is changed, which further limits its interpretation. The shift in current baseline around 27 July suggests that any setup changes should be recorded for each session.

Studies that measure photovoltaic output while a vehicle is moving record both irradiance and the module's electrical response \citep{ref-wetzel,ref-ng,ref-ota-irradiance,ref-ota-driving}. Photovoltaic monitoring standards also specify the variables and measurement points that should be defined \citep{ref-iec61724}. Irradiance and module temperature were not recorded in this study, and the current channels have fixed limits and zero readings. The sensor locations, polarity, and calibration are undocumented, so these records alone cannot identify the causes of the observed problems. Table~\ref{tab:hallazgos} turns these measurement gaps into a minimum instrumentation protocol for future studies of solar contributions to traction. A standardized energy-consumption and range test \citep{ref-sae1634} would be meaningful only after the measurement chain has been verified.

\subsection{Limitations}

This study includes one vehicle, eight sessions with adequate data coverage in July and August 2026, and two route types. Because the sessions were not controlled replicates, the findings cannot be generalized to other vehicles, routes, or seasons. The sensor calibration and polarity, the actual measurement schematic, the vehicle mass, and the algorithms used to calculate speed and distance are unknown. Earlier movement-based filtering and the exclusion criteria also affect the dataset; using a 100-record threshold does not remove the resulting risk of selection bias. The block-bootstrap CIs account for temporal dependence, but comparisons based on only eight sessions are indicative. Specific-energy indicators and the empirical slope rely on signals with unknown calibration. Gel-battery capacity and aging were not evaluated \citep{ref-ruetschi}; irradiance, temperature, and state of charge were not measured. As a result, the data do not support conclusions about MPPT performance, solar contribution to traction, regenerative recovery, cost savings, or emissions reductions.

\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Of 4277 movement records, 3052 records across 11 dates were retained; eight sessions (3029 records) supported between-session analysis. Zero battery-voltage readings accounted for 46.7\% of exclusions.
    \item Prototype use comprised a repeated route of approximately 1.4 km (mean speed, 14.7--16.0 km/h) and confined circuits (7.7--8.7 km/h), with moderate acceleration (5th and 95th percentiles: $\pm 0.40$ m/s$^2$) and speeds predominantly below 20 km/h.
    \item The reported speed is derived from GPS coordinates; no independent validation is available.
    \item Motor-current readings are limited to $\pm 6.55$ A, their baseline varies by session, and they show no relationship with speed or acceleration. Panel-channel current is limited to $\pm 3.28$ A, and 34\% of its readings are zero. The current measurement chain does not support estimates of energy consumption or photovoltaic generation.
    \item The observed measurement failures translate into specific instrumentation requirements: an appropriate current range, zero-offset and polarity verification, measurements at the MPPT input and output, synchronized irradiance, an independent speed reference, and continuous logging with error codes.
\end{enumerate}
